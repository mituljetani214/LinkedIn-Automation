## Approved Caption

AI will not fix a broken workflow.

It will only help you move faster inside that broken workflow.

That is why I do not like starting product design with screens, prompts, or visual exploration.

I prefer to start with:

- Who is the user?
- What are they trying to finish?
- Where do they slow down?
- What information do they need?
- What decision are they trying to make?
- What can be automated safely?
- What still needs human judgment?

This matters even more now because AI is becoming part of the design and product workflow itself.

Figma is moving AI, motion, code, and reusable workflows closer to the canvas. Enterprise products are also moving toward operator-first experiences where AI sits inside the working surface, not beside it.

But the core design question is still the same:

Does this help a real person complete a real task better?

In Munim, the biggest improvement did not start from making the UI look modern.

It started from measuring a workflow.

Invoice creation was taking approximately 1 minute 38 seconds. After redesigning the flow, simplifying the structure, and making the experience more keyboard-friendly, the task came down to under 30 seconds.

That is the kind of problem AI should help us solve.

Not just faster screens.

Better workflows.
